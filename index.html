Launch Implications · Korea
What the snapshot means for the analogue + pricing machinery downstream
Signal 01 · Reimbursement Bar
Korea's NHIS spine carries 57–62% of national health spend — comparable to US Medicare/Medicaid share, well below Japan (86%) or Australia (74%). HIRA listing is the single biggest commercial gate, but if it fails the OOP runway is real.
Signal 02 · OOP Capacity
Korea's OOP share (29–34%) is ~2× the OECD avg. Structural feature, not a bug — patients are accustomed to paying out-of-pocket for premium care. This is why Mounjaro can launch self-pay despite the NHIS gap on anti-obesity.
Signal 03 · Spend Trajectory
CHE per capita grew 5.0% CAGR over the last decade, lifting Korea from $1.9k to $3.1k — outpacing Japan (flat-to-negative) and tracking Singapore. Payer has headroom, but pricing scrutiny on innovative agents is rising in step.
Signal 04 · Voluntary Private
Voluntary private insurance is ~8% of CHE — small but growing. Useful as a bridge channel for premium oral therapies (CSU, autoimmune) where NHIS coverage is gated by step-therapy.

Korea allocates 21.2% of CHE to pharmaceuticals + medical goods — the highest share in the comparator set, ~50% above the OECD-tier average (~14–18%). This is structural: Korea's NHIS is fee-for-service with a positive-list formulary that funnels spend through the drug channel. A pharma launch in Korea operates inside a system already pre-disposed to pharmaceutical spend — favorable for category expansion, but it also explains HIRA's aggressive pricing-negotiation posture.
METHODOLOGY NOTE · Disease-specific spend allocation (NCD / CVD / cancer / diabetes) is reported in GHED dis4_che primarily for donor-tracked LMIC programs. OECD-tier markets (KR/JP/AUS/US/SG) do not consistently disaggregate to that grain in SHA 2011 returns, so this view uses the care-function axis (HC) as the closest disease-relevant proxy. Pharma share is the most actionable signal for a drug-launch lens.
Payer-Mix Waterfall · Drug Spend Decomposition
For every ₩100,000 of HIRA-listed price · scenario-toggled by NHIS coverage status
Scenario A · NHIS-Listed
e.g., Tirzepatide for T2D · Rhapsido post-HIRA
Net OOP ₩28,000 per ₩100k list
Standard NHIS outpatient drug formula: 70% scheme / 30% patient co-pay. For Type 2 Diabetes (chronic registration) co-pay drops to 30% at clinics. Voluntary private indemnity rarely covers drug co-pays meaningfully.
Scenario B · Out of Formulary
e.g., Mounjaro (obesity, current) · Rhapsido pre-HIRA
Net OOP ₩95,000 per ₩100k list
Full patient liability falls on the household. Voluntary private indemnity (실손보험) occasionally reimburses a small fraction (~5%) when the prescription is medically indicated. Demand still real — Korea's 34% OOP-share infrastructure makes self-pay launches commercially viable.
Net patient burden swings 3.4× between formulary states (₩28k → ₩95k per ₩100k list). For molecules with reimbursement uncertainty, the Timing Forecast's "Korea Reimbursement Date" circle isn't cosmetic — it's the single biggest commercial-model lever.
